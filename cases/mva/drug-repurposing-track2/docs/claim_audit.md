# Load-bearing claim audit

This audit checks source scope, not clinical truth in the child. The full ledger is `data/evidence.csv`. Quotes below are short retrieved passages; proposed causal extensions remain visible in `data/edges.csv`.

| Claim checked | Verdict | Supporting passage / location | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Public Asn1002Lys record is uncertain, not verified patient pathogenic genotype | Supported for retrieved record | ClinVar VCV004600147: “Uncertain significance”; title `NM_001211.6(BUB1B):c.3006T>A (p.Asn1002Lys)` [E02] | Preserve DNA-identity/phase uncertainty |
| Human K668 is a real residue in the retained segment | Supported by reference and primary mechanism | UniProt reference sequence at 668 is lysine; SIRT2 paper: “Deacetylation of lysine 668 (K668) of BubR1 inhibits ubiquitylation” [E04,E09] | Site exists, but exact-mutant rescue remains untested |
| SIRT2/NMN evidence proves drug rescue of case alleles | Unsupported extension | SIRT2 paper reports stability and model-specific abundance/survival experiments, not either supplied allele [E09,E10] | No such claim in report; full chain has gaps |
| Localized B56 recruitment can rescue a model alignment defect | Supported in cited models | “artificially targeted PP2A-B56s to the kinetochores was able to rescue chromosome misalignment in MVA patient cell lines” [E12, discussion] | Retain strongest bypass experiment, not a medication |
| Alignment rescue proves faithful divisions | Unsupported extension | Source uses congression/fiber assays, including MG132 context [E12,E13] | Require live completed divisions and selection controls |
| Rapamycin is proven to restore tissue in the 2026 fly model | Unsupported extension | “A similar result was obtained by feeding larvae with the TOR inhibitor Rapamycin”; surrounding endpoint is neuroblast number [E43 p12] | Cell preservation only |
| Related genetic TOR/autophagy manipulations failed distal tissue endpoints | Supported in cited model | “none of the genetic interventions aimed at increasing autophagy was able to rescue the number of Pros+ cells or the size of the larval brain” [E43 pp12-13] | Do not equate survival and tissue benefit |
| Arimoclomol is a universal mutant-protein chaperone rescue | Contradicted as generalized assertion | “we did not see correction of cholesterol storage by arimoclomol ... at doses up to 0.5 mM” [E48 published PMC abstract/results] | Context-dependent, not proven for BUBR1 |
| Arimoclomol's approved clinical effect establishes a precise HSP/BUBR1 mechanism | Unsupported extension | Label: “The mechanism(s) by which arimoclomol exerts its clinical effects in patients with NPC is unknown.” [E51 section12.1] | Keep experimental HSP anchor distinct from clinical MoA |
| Potassium citrate is evidence-based for all congenital nephrocalcinosis | Unsupported extension | Label: “The pivotal Urocit-K trials were non-randomized and non-placebo controlled”; “Safety and effectiveness in children have not been established.” [E35 sections14,8.4] | Conditional chemistry/formulation branch only |
| Larotrectinib targets TRK | Supported | “Larotrectinib is an inhibitor of the tropomyosin receptor kinases (TRK), TRKA, TRKB, and TRKC.” [E40 p15] | Still requires an actual oncogenic tumor fusion/dependency |
| Metformin uniformly raises BUBR1 | Not supported as uniform | Same study reports BUBR1/MAD2 *deregulation* with glucose-dependent genotoxic stress; the effect is cell-type dependent and bidirectional (raised in some, lowered in others) [E23] | Frame metformin as bidirectional, context-dependent; direction is the weakest link |
| Arimoclomol activates HSP90 to refold BUBR1 | Unsupported extension | Arimoclomol is a heat-shock-response co-inducer (HSF1 → HSP70/HSP90), not an HSP90 inhibitor; no BUBR1 bridge, and one NPC study showed no HSP70 induction [E47, E48] | Keep as a stability hypothesis, not a demonstrated refolding mechanism |
| Hydroxychloroquine increases functional BUBR1 | Supported only as senescence-specific level increase | HCQ raised BubR1 only in late-passage senescent cells by blocking lysosomal degradation; everolimus lowered it [E54] | Degradation-blocking, not synthesis or proven function; neuromuscular caution |
| Amikacin is a safer aminoglycoside for read-through | Partly supported / cautioned | CPIC: avoid aminoglycosides in MT-RNR1 m.1555A>G/m.1494C>T/m.1095T>C carriers; no intra-class safety ranking supported [E53] | Screen MT-RNR1 first; read-through for p.Leu737* unconfirmed |
| Olaparib is indicated by BUB1B-driven instability | Unsupported extension | CIN is not homologous-recombination deficiency; olaparib is HRR/BRCA-selected with no RMS/BUB1B indication [E24, E55] | Requires a demonstrated tumor repair dependency |
| Gentamicin reporter concentration was 800 mg/mL | Contradicted transcription artifact | Original journal HTML uses 800 micrograms/mL; PDF extraction corrupted the micro symbol [E18 methods] | Correct units retained; no child-dose conversion |
| No sufficiently supported constitutional approved candidate identified | Coverage-limited synthesis | No retrieved path joins exact alleles, functional rescue, feasible exposure and tumor-safe tissue benefit | Not a claim of exhaustive absence or evidence of no effect |

The most consequential limitations are the exact-allele functional gap, absence of a normal-versus-tumor window, and tissue/exposure transfers. Additional citations to the same first mechanistic step would not close them.
