## B. Extended variant mechanism and genotype branches

### Public mapping versus case identity

The script verifies the public ClinVar nucleotide substitutions against RefSeq sequence and independently reconciles their chromosome positions through Ensembl exon intervals. It does not use a protein label to guess a DNA allele. Transcript length agreement is checked; the original laboratory transcript still takes precedence for case reconciliation. `data/reference_context.json` records reference codon, flanking sequence, exon number and explicit patient-identity uncertainty.

ClinVar VCV000533901 contains multiple submitted pathogenic/likely pathogenic assertions; VCV004600147 contains a single-submitter VUS assertion. Underlying clinical segregation details are insufficient to phase this case. The public stop record exposes several population-database annotations in `variation_set`; these are preserved verbatim in `data/population_annotations.json`, without combining releases into an invented frequency. An empty annotation list for another record does not mean absent from gnomAD. The current gnomAD browser/API allele record was not independently established in this search. [E01–E03]

### p.Leu737Ter

- **NMD:** the public matching stop lies before multiple downstream junctions. This supports predicted NMD under the usual exon-junction model, not measured RNA destruction. Assess allele-specific RNA in native transcripts with and without a research NMD perturbation, with a known NMD-sensitive control. Tissue, translation and stress state can alter the effect.
- **Retained sequence:** N-terminal interaction regions and the KARD region precede the stop; the C-terminal annotated kinase-like domain is lost. Kinase-domain annotation is not evidence that an activating kinase drug would repair BUBR1. Binding, scaffolding, localization and checkpoint timing must be assayed separately. [E04, E07, E12, E20]
- **Residual product:** an expressed truncated protein could supply useful scaffolding, sequester partners, mislocalize, or be unstable. The BUBR1(1–730) construct result motivates a test but cannot establish any of these outcomes for an endogenous 1–736 product. [E12]
- **Readthrough:** verify native stop and flanks, transcript abundance, product size and the inserted peptide. Near-cognate insertion need not recreate leucine. Require rescue of localization/interactions and completed-division fidelity. Native/minigene assays are needed because intronless cDNA expression removes NMD context. [E18, E46]

### p.Asn1002Lys

No retrieved exact-variant experiment established reduced abundance, destabilization, altered folding, binding, localization, checkpoint signaling, attachment control, a splice defect, or preserved function. These remain competing possibilities. Domain membership provides a hypothesis, not a prediction of pathogenicity or a drug target. Do not transfer effects of neighboring residues or a generic missense score to this allele. An exonic nucleotide change can affect splicing independently of its annotated amino acid; resolve the actual nucleotide and test RNA before considering splice correction. No splice-correcting approved medication was found for a demonstrated defect here. [E02–E04, E07]

### Phase and additional findings

- **In trans:** one allele may undergo NMD while the other supplies most full-length protein; residual-function testing is decisive.
- **In cis:** the upstream stop may prevent routine translation of the downstream missense site on that same allele. Readthrough could then produce a full-length protein that still carries Asn1002Lys. The second homolog may be WT, carry a missed allele/CNV, or have altered expression. Revisit the recessive model rather than assuming compound heterozygosity.
- **Unknown phase:** retain both branches; do not presume a WT allele is available for upregulation.
- **Additional diagnosis/structural variant:** renal or muscle features may have another cause; laboratory re-review should cover the assay's blind spots without forcing a single-gene explanation.
- **Tissue-specific expression or mosaicism:** blood findings and cultured fibroblasts may not predict renal, muscle, progenitor or tumor states. Record passage, culture selection and karyotype drift.

## C. Mechanism landscape: axes A to I

### Axis A: Allele-specific restoration

**Hypothesis (strategy 1):** restoring native stop-allele translation produces useful BUBR1. **Required state:** correct stop context, sufficient RNA, functional readthrough product. **Anchors:** context-sensitive gentamicin reporters; macrolide/APC experiments; public stop sequence. **Approved entry points:** azithromycin, amikacin and gentamicin Tier3. Azithromycin is attractive because macrolides read through premature stops at roughly 100-fold lower concentration than gentamicin [E46]; amikacin may carry comparatively lower nephrotoxicity but, like all aminoglycosides, requires `MT-RNR1` screening (m.1555A>G, m.1494C>T, m.1095T>C) before use because carriers face a high risk of aminoglycoside-induced hearing loss [E53]. **Missing links:** exact-allele readthrough, inserted residue, correctly localized protein, fidelity, safe exposure. **Counterarguments:** NMD limits substrate; reporter signal can be artifactual; longer protein may fail; antibiotic concentrations at ribosomes are not whole-tissue accumulation; chronic renal/auditory/mitochondrial or QT/hepatic effects matter. **Test:** native transcript with orthogonal peptide and functional assays. **Stop:** no functional rescue before toxicity/exposure limits. Broad NMD inhibition is a laboratory probe only; global RNA-surveillance disruption and harmful truncated products prevent a systemic rationale. Splicing correction remains closed until an actual splice defect is demonstrated. [E01–E03, E18, E28, E30, E46, E50, E53]

### Axis B: Preserve or increase residual functional BUBR1

**Hypothesis (strategy 2):** a useful but unstable allele can be stabilized, or its degradation slowed, to raise *functional* BUBR1. **Required state:** equal-expression complementation shows useful function; native protein half-life/localization are limiting. **Anchors:** allele-specific MVA defects; SIRT2-K668 stability; HSP-directed NPC experiments as remote context; autophagy-dependent BUBR1 turnover. **Approved entry points:** arimoclomol, hydroxychloroquine, metformin, trametinib and prescription niacin ER Tier3; broad proteasome inhibition (bortezomib) TierX. **Missing links:** BUBR1-specific chaperone dependence or selective NAD/SIRT2 response and mutant fidelity. **Contradictions and cautions:** arimoclomol is a heat-shock-response *co-inducer* (HSF1 → HSP70/HSP90), not an HSP90 inhibitor, and it did not induce HSP70/correct storage in another NPC cell study; FDA states its clinical NPC mechanism is unknown. Metformin changed BUBR1 **bidirectionally**: raised in some cell types and lowered in others within the same study, alongside glucose-dependent genotoxic stress [E23]. Hydroxychloroquine raised BUBR1 only in senescent cells by blocking autophagic/lysosomal degradation (not synthesis), and the autophagy inducer everolimus lowered it [E54]. Trametinib's rationale inverts a finding that BubR1 *deficiency* enhances MEK/ERK, so it is explicitly theoretical [E39]. SIRT2-K250 participates in checkpoint silencing; more deacetylation is not uniformly beneficial. **Test:** native protein turnover, K668/K250 state, localization and fidelity with target-dependence controls. **Stop:** protein-only increase, wrong localization, partner sequestration or tumor protection. No approved selective BUBR1 stabilizer/SIRT2 activator was established. [E07, E09–E11, E23, E37, E39, E47–E49, E51–E52, E54]

### Axis C: Specific BUBR1 functions

**Hypothesis:** correct the demonstrated interaction defect rather than total abundance. **Required state:** distinguish MCC formation/BUB3 interaction/CDC20-APC-C regulation from PP2A-B56 attachment control. **Anchors:** domain complementation, B56-binding mutants and kinetochore-tethered rescue. **Approved entry point (strategy 3):** fingolimod is carried at Tier3 as an explicitly speculative PP2A-reactivation probe. It activates PP2A in *bulk* by antagonising the SET/I2PP2A inhibitor; no approved agent produces selective kinetochore B56 recruitment or MCC reconstruction, and fingolimod's approved S1P activity and the broader PP2A literature do not establish spatial recruitment. **Test:** co-immunoprecipitation/proximity readouts, kinetochore occupancy, cyclin-B/securin dynamics, cold-stable fibers and unperturbed divisions. **Stop:** only global phosphatase activity or prolonged arrest; no improved fidelity. [E07, E12–E14, E20, E36]

### Axis D: Functional compensation/bypass

**Hypothesis:** rebalance attachment kinases/phosphatases without restoring BUBR1. **Required state:** attachment-control defect with a tractable opposing pathway. **Anchors:** targeted B56 rescue; partial Aurora-B-inhibitor rescue; U0126/ERK inhibitor rescue in RAS/MAPK-hyperactive cells. **Approved entry:** trametinib only as a conditional drug-substitution experiment. **Missing links:** excessive MAPK in normal case cells, clinically relevant drug effect, faithful divisions, window over tumor. **Counterarguments:** Aurora B is necessary for error correction; PLK1 inhibition did not rescue in the cited study and produced spindle abnormalities. Prolonged mitosis is not correction. MEK inhibition may improve malignant-cell mitotic fitness. **Test:** compare genetic bypass, research inhibitor, approved candidate and rescue-resistant/orthogonal perturbations. **Stop:** alignment without faithful anaphase, arrest, cell loss, or equal normal-cell toxicity. [E12–E14, E19, E39]

### Axis E: Downstream genome/cellular injury

**Hypothesis:** a demonstrated consequence of complex aneuploidy is modifiable even if segregation errors persist. **Anchors:** BubR1-low oxidative-challenge sensitivity; fly SAC-loss proteostasis/mitochondrial dysfunction and genetic rescues. **Required state:** show the injury in relevant BUB1B normal tissue, not merely ROS or damage in an unrelated cancer. **Approved entry:** sirolimus is now deprioritised to TierX (fly cell-preservation signal only; preliminary negative mouse report); no sufficiently supported approved mitochondrial/chaperone/ROS-scavenger equivalent was identified. **Missing links:** which injury mediates which tissue phenotype, and whether a drug reproduces genetic rescue without preserving tumor cells. **Counterarguments:** DNA-damage pathways were not the main explanation of stemness loss in the fly model; CIN is not HR deficiency. Metformin's high-dose low-glucose genotoxic signal is context-specific contrary evidence, not proof of harm at usual exposure. **Test:** cell-state-matched replication-fork, mitochondrial, redox and proteostasis measures followed by orthogonal causal perturbation; productive tissue output. **Stop:** marker reduction without functional benefit; therapy antagonism; no causal injury. PARP inhibitors are not prioritized from 'genomic instability' alone. [E23–E24, E43–E45]

### Axis F: Senescence, inflammation and tissue dysfunction

**Hypothesis:** senescent cells or stress-associated signaling drive a specific tissue deficit. **Anchors:** INK-ATTAC clearance in BubR1H/H mice; tissue-dependent p21/p53 effects. **Approved entry:** sirolimus and dasatinib both TierX (deprioritised) in this context. **Missing links:** harmful senescent population, selective pharmacologic removal or useful SASP suppression, productive tissue regeneration. cGAS-STING activation was not demonstrated for the reported genotype in retrieved evidence; micronuclei alone do not establish it. **Counterarguments:** genetic cell deletion is not a senolytic drug; senescence can restrain tumors; anti-inflammatory intervention may suppress antitumor immunity; progenitor deletion and growth inhibition matter in childhood. The preliminary rapamycin-null statement is low-rigor contrary evidence, not a definitive trial. **Test:** multiple senescence markers and persistent state, secretion assays, functional tissue coculture and selective perturbation. If cGAS/STING activation is measured, use genetic epistasis before any drug hypothesis. **Stop:** protective senescence lost, immune harm, progenitor injury, or inflammation reduced without tissue improvement. [E15–E17, E31, E33, E43]

### Axis G: Tissue-specific support

**Renal hypothesis:** appropriate mineral/acid-base correction can reduce new deposition independently of BUB1B. **Required facts:** nephrocalcinosis timing/distribution; prematurity exposures including loop diuretics; hypercalciuria, hypocitraturia, oxalate, urine pH, acid-base state, calcium/phosphate/PTH/vitamin-D context as indicated, and tubular/filtration function. **Entry:** potassium citrate ER is deprioritised to TierX pending a defined citrate-responsive biochemical diagnosis and a feasible pediatric formulation. Hypercalciuria-directed or inherited hyperoxaluria therapies require their own diagnoses and regulatory review; they were not elevated into the shortlist without those facts. **Test:** nephrology-directed biochemical classification, mineral-specific supersaturation and experimental renal/crystal response if needed. **Stop:** no biochemical match, unsafe potassium handling or unfavorable calcium-phosphate supersaturation. [E35]

**Muscle/growth hypothesis:** reduced muscle mass and poor growth may reflect nutrition, prematurity, endocrine abnormalities, disuse, tumor burden or therapy rather than primary atrophy. Document strength, motor function and pre-treatment trajectory; use CK/metabolic/endocrine investigations only when clinically indicated. No approved BUB1B-specific muscle/growth medication was found. Prescription niacin evidence in adult mitochondrial myopathy does not establish this diagnosis; somatropin is contraindicated with active malignancy. A muscle model becomes justified only after a demonstrable muscle-level defect. [E38, E49, E52]

### Axis H: Tumor vulnerabilities

**Hypothesis:** exploit a measured tumor dependency while sparing constitutional normal tissues. **Required state:** pathology/subtype; FOXO1 classification as relevant; tumor sequence/fusion/CNV profile; germline/tumor allele state; therapy history. **Entry (strategy 4):** larotrectinib Tier2 for a true NTRK-fusion dependency and matching clinical context (not NTRK expression or amplification alone); vincristine Tier2 as the standard RMS cytotoxic backbone with MVA-aware dose modification [E22]; olaparib Tier3 only if a tumor homologous-recombination/repair dependency is demonstrated: chromosomal instability alone does not imply HRD, and a PARP indication does not follow from BUB1B loss [E24, E55]. **Other searches:** temsirolimus trial evidence, MAPK-related BUBR1 dependency and generic CIN vulnerabilities were reviewed. **Missing links:** no tumor molecular data and no normal-cell window. DepMap/PRISM/GDSC sensitivity, if later accessed, is only a discovery aid. **Counterarguments:** checkpoint inhibition, mitotic stress, proteasome injury and DNA-damage targeting may hit affected normal tissues at least as hard; aneuploidy does not imply HRD. **Test:** matched tumor/normal panels, independent target perturbation, exposure/time dependence and current-therapy antagonism. **Stop:** no dependency, equal normal toxicity or tumor rescue by a constitutional-cell intervention. [E19, E21–E24, E32, E40–E42, E55]

### Axis I: Combinations/sequences

No clinical combination is proposed. A **research** sequence is permissible only after single mechanisms work:

1. NMD-limited substrate followed by readthrough: use a factorial design to establish whether substrate availability genuinely enables functional rescue, not merely a larger dysfunctional band. Broad NMD blockade may be harmful.
2. Readthrough followed by protein-folding support: only if the readthrough product is demonstrably unstable yet functional at matched abundance. Test antagonism and total proteotoxic burden.
3. Correct segregation first, then address persistent tissue injury: distinguish prevention of new aneuploidy from surviving old aneuploid cells. Never claim reversal of an established tissue phenotype from an upstream assay.

All remain below supported single-mechanism hypotheses. Separate tumor and normal models are mandatory before considering translational compatibility. No combination advancement without feasible individual exposures, a genuine enabling effect, and absence of treatment antagonism.

## D. Tier rules and exposure assessment

The exact candidate table is [candidates.csv](../data/candidates.csv). A single major mechanistic, exposure or tumor-safety failure can reject a candidate; publication count and graph degree do not compensate.

- **Tier1:** genotype-compatible functional rescue in an appropriate model, substantially complete causal chain, plausible exposure, no invalidating unresolved safety conflict. Empty.
- **Tier2:** bounded decisive gaps with a plausible translational path. Here the tumour-directed options qualify conditionally (larotrectinib on a confirmed NTRK fusion, vincristine as the standard RMS backbone), contingent on tumour molecular findings and clinical context, not constitutional MVA rescue.
- **Tier3:** multi-link transfer, absent exact-genotype function, uncertain exposure or exploratory tissue mechanism.
- **TierX:** wrong direction, no meaningful bridge, major safety/window problem, or contradicted generalized benefit claim. Reopening criteria remain recorded.

Evidence dimensions are independent: **rigor** (design/controls), **causal directness** (perturbation and rescue versus association), **variant/model relevance**, **exposure feasibility**, **reproducibility** (independent versus same family), **clinical-context relevance**. High/moderate/low/unknown/not-applicable refer to each dimension; they are not averaged. Abstract-only access limits rigor assessment. Regulatory records are strong sources for approved use and warnings, not molecular rescue experiments.

**Exposure bridge not completed for any constitutional candidate.** Compare eventual effective *unbound intracellular* concentrations and duration with human pharmacokinetics, tissue distribution, protein binding, metabolite activity and pediatric clearance. Nominal dish concentration, fly-food concentration and total tissue accumulation cannot be substituted for that bridge. Published values retained for context include gentamicin 800 micrograms/mL in reporters, NMN 1 mM for 24 h in MEFs, Aurora tool ZM447439 2 micromolar for 1 h under arrest, and rapamycin 40 micromolar in fly food for about 48 h. These are source-reported experimental conditions, exported by `scripts/build_package.py`; none is converted to a child's dose. [E10, E13, E18, E43]

## H. Minimum discriminating experimental program

### H1: Highest information gain: allele-resolved function

**Materials:** authorized patient-derived normal cells if available, independent isogenic corrected clones and parental controls where authorized; otherwise a clearly labeled engineered model of the *verified nucleotide alleles*. Do not engineer a guessed protein-equivalent nucleotide variant. Include WT/WT, each allele separately, the phased pair, BUB1B-deficiency control and corrected WT benchmark. Native genomic editing preserves NMD/splicing; complementary inducible cDNA expression tests protein function but cannot assess NMD.

**Stage 1, native output:** allele-specific RNA with transcript-junction assays and an NMD-sensitive positive control; NMD perturbation as a short research diagnostic only. N- and C-terminal protein detection plus targeted proteomics distinguish full-length and truncated species. Measure stability and kinetochore occupancy normalized to mitotic cell state. Test splice junctions rather than assuming the missense annotation exhausts the consequence.

**Stage 2, function at matched expression:** complement each allele at physiologically calibrated abundance; compare WT correction. Assay BUB3/CDC20/MAD2 complex formation and cyclin-B/securin destruction timing; separately B56 localization, K-fibers and attachment correction. Short drug-induced checkpoint challenges can diagnose function, but primary rescue measurement occurs in unperturbed completed divisions.

**Primary endpoint:** fraction of completed mitoses with prospectively defined lagging chromosome/bridge/missegregation events, assessed by blinded live-cell imaging. Report total entry into mitosis, completion, arrest, death, generation time and daughter-cell fate separately. Confirm a subset with single-cell chromosome measurements; micronuclei alone are secondary. Track lineages to distinguish fewer new errors from removal of existing abnormal cells.

**Prospective proposed advancement criterion, not observed evidence:** a candidate must close at least half of the mutant-versus-corrected excess error rate, with a confidence interval excluding no improvement, without a greater-than-10% decrease in completed divisions or viable daughter output. These are screening gates requiring pilot refinement before unblinding, not clinically meaningful benefit thresholds. If corrected cells do not establish a reproducible assay deficit, do not compute a rescue fraction or screen on noise.

Define the proposed rescue fraction as `(p_vehicle - p_candidate) / (p_vehicle - p_corrected)`, where each probability is the new-error rate per completed division in the matched biological run. Require a positive, reproducible denominator; report absolute risk difference alongside this fraction and do not truncate negative or greater-than-one results. Arrest and death remain separate outcomes, never removed to improve the numerator.

**Reproducibility/statistics:** at least three independent culture/imaging runs and two independent edited clones where feasible; biological run/clone is the unit, cells are nested observations. Pre-specify one primary contrast; use a binomial mixed model or cluster-bootstrap interval for per-division errors. Report raw effect, confidence interval and multiplicity-adjusted exploratory comparisons separately; use Holm adjustment for the limited predeclared mechanistic contrasts. Determine sample size from blinded pilot run/clone variability and baseline event rate rather than fabricating a power estimate. No p-value has been computed for proposed experiments.

**Invalidation:** WT correction fails to normalize the assumed defect; native allele identity differs; phenotype arises only from overexpression/culture selection; 'rescue' comes from arrest/death; or improved normal-cell fidelity accompanies tumor protection.

### H2: Discriminate stabilization from attachment bypass

Rank next because it can eliminate whole drug classes cheaply. Compare expression-matched missense/truncation rescue with kinetochore-targeted B56, binding-defective B56/BUBR1 controls and untethered B56. If tethering improves alignment, require subsequent completed-division fidelity and sustained viable progeny. If protein increase works but tethering does not, prioritize native stability/localization assays. If tethering works despite nonfunctional allele output, bypass is favored. If both fail while WT correction works, another BUBR1 function is limiting.

**Drug mapping:** arimoclomol/niacin follow only an abundance/stability-limited branch; trametinib follows only a demonstrated MAPK-dependent branch. Compare target perturbation genetically and pharmacologically, including relevant inactive/binding-defective controls. Avoid concluding that generic proteasome inhibition or bulk PP2A activity reproduces a spatially specific rescue.

### H3: Native stop readthrough

Use the verified allele with its introns/flanks; include WT sense construct, stop construct, translation/measurement controls and a native RNA abundance readout. Compare azithromycin to gentamicin as a mechanistic benchmark at analytically measured exposures, not a clinical regimen. Identify the readthrough peptide across the stop, quantify full-length product and test function under H1. A rise in C-terminal antibody signal alone is not sufficient. Test NMD rescue separately; a factorial experiment only follows evidence that substrate is limiting. Stop at a nonfunctional product or an implausible exposure window. Current corrections/full-method gaps in macrolide literature must be resolved before relying on its numerical pharmacology.

### H4: Tissue-specific downstream discrimination

Only use a tissue model justified by a documented phenotype. In a demonstrated proteostasis/mitochondrial defect, compare genetic mitochondrial chaperone/ROS-scavenger support, appropriate autophagy perturbations and sirolimus. Measure flux rather than static LC3/lysosome accumulation, respiration/redox with cell-number controls, lineage output and actual tissue function. The fly result predefines the failure mode: **more surviving progenitors without productive tissue output is not tissue rescue**. In renal support, biochemical classification and mineral-specific supersaturation precede organoid pharmacology. Established deposits, new deposition and filtration are separate endpoints.

### H5: Tumor and exposure gates

Current RMS tissue/organoid or a justified molecularly matched model must be compared with constitutionally affected normal cells, including a vulnerable tissue when indicated. Tumor subtype/fusion/CNV and target-dependence controls are required. Test effects on tumor growth, clonogenic recovery and the actual anticancer regimen once known; do not invent a drug combination. Model clinically plausible free-exposure/time windows, with renal, mitochondrial, marrow and immune relevance. Define failure as no selectivity, normal-progenitor loss, tumor protection, therapy antagonism or a required exposure outside established human safety constraints.

## I. Up to three mechanism-distinct submission concepts

### Concept 1: Native-allele rescue with a functional gate

**Hypothesis:** one allele supplies a rescuable product; its limiting step can be distinguished experimentally. **Fit:** directly addresses the stop/missense pair without presuming two trans alleles or a WT allele. **Evidence:** native context/NMD inference, variant-dependent BUBR1 dysfunction and nonBUB1B readthrough/chaperone anchors. **Candidate:** azithromycin only for a verified readthrough-compatible branch; arimoclomol only for a proven instability branch; both Tier3, not coadministration. **Gaps:** native product, phase, inserted residue, functional gain and exposure. **Validation:** H1 followed by H3 or the relevant stability test, not both by default. **Safety barrier:** tumor fitness plus antibiotic/chaperone systemic effects. **Novelty:** selection by allele-level failure mode, not BUB1B-expression signature. **Scalability:** reusable native-transcript/isogenic assay; each allele still needs validation. **Limit:** no exact-allele drug rescue presently demonstrated.

### Concept 2: Localization-specific attachment bypass

**Hypothesis:** deficient local PP2A-B56 activity drives a demonstrable attachment defect even when total protein manipulation fails. **Fit:** putative truncation retains the recruitment region in sequence; MVA-model tethering offers a causal reference. **Evidence:** genetic B56 targeting, binding-defective controls and chromosome-alignment rescue. **Candidate:** no approved selective recruitment drug found; trametinib is a separate conditional bridge only with excessive MAPK. **Validation:** H2 plus completed-division fidelity and target epistasis. **Safety barrier:** excessive attachment stabilization or tumor mitotic protection. **Novelty:** spatial function rather than generic kinase or abundance modulation. **Scalability:** recruitment/fidelity screening can identify new compounds, but a screening platform is not an approved-drug answer. **Limit:** alignment and druggable localization remain decisive gaps.

### Concept 3: Productive tissue rescue, not aneuploid-cell preservation

**Hypothesis:** a documented mitochondrial/proteostasis consequence is more tractable than the initiating segregation defect. **Fit:** independent of which allele carries residual function, but requires a relevant tissue lesion. **Evidence:** 2026 SAC-loss fly genetics distinguish mitochondrial support from TOR-associated cell preservation; BubR1-low oxidant sensitivity supplies a related, narrower anchor. **Candidate:** sirolimus as a deprioritised (TierX) comparator only; no justified approved equivalent of mitochondrial genetic rescue yet. **Validation:** H4, comparing productive progeny and tissue function rather than cell counts; maintain H5 tumor gate. **Safety barrier:** preserving damaged/premalignant cells, immunosuppression and developmental growth inhibition. **Novelty:** explicitly separates survival from useful tissue output and from chromosome correction. **Scalability:** flux/lineage assays across aneuploid disorders, with genotype/tissue revalidation. **Limit:** fly bub3/rod neural data do not establish BUB1B muscle/renal benefit.

No numerical hackathon score is assigned. If the rubric is later used, first exclude proposals failing mechanistic/safety gates; then anchor scientific rigor to controls and falsifiability, impact to the demonstrated unmet endpoint, innovation to a resolvable new mechanism, and scalability to reproducible assays. The 35/25/25/15 weights must not be interpreted as probability of treatment success.

## J. Reproducibility, privacy and final quality check

Run `python scripts/normalize_references.py`, `python scripts/build_package.py`, then `python scripts/validate_package.py` from a fresh process. Standard library only; no patient files or network required for regeneration. Original extraction decisions reside in `curation/`; manual report interpretation is not generated by a language model at rerun. `data/search_log.csv` records selected discovery queries and cited-source retrievals, with source access and limitations. `data/validation.json` reports final schemas, reference integrity, counts and file hashes.

The GitHub layout is `docs/` (manuscript), `data/` (ledgers), `curation/` (JSON inputs), `reference/` (public snapshots) and `scripts/`. Optionally add future **de-identified, authorized** assay summaries and notebooks in separate versioned analysis folders. Keep controlled genomic data, pedigree identifiers, medical records, consent material and raw patient files outside the repository. Do not open-license third-party full texts or restricted datasets. No public push was performed; this package is intended for the research team and requires a publication/privacy review before any release because a rare phenotype combination can itself be identifying.
